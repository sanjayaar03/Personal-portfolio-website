What to Prepare Before Running Facebook and Instagram Ads

A clear offer, a useful landing page, and sensible measurement come before the launch button.

By Sanjay Ranpal · Digital Sanjaya

Define one useful business objective

Decide what you want the campaign to help people do: make an enquiry, book a conversation, or purchase a specific product. Choose an objective your business can support and a way to assess the quality of the response. A campaign becomes easier to evaluate when its purpose is clear.

Make your offer easy to explain

Write down who the offer is for, what problem it addresses, what is included, and what happens next. Check that any price, availability, or eligibility information is accurate. Ads cannot resolve confusion in the offer itself.

Prepare the destination

Open your landing page on a phone. Does it match the promise in the ad? Is the next step obvious? Test the form, confirmation message, contact information, and page speed. Remove unnecessary fields and make sure someone is responsible for responding to enquiries.

Check account access and measurement

Confirm the right people have access to your business and advertising assets. Review conversion tracking where appropriate and ensure any consent requirements are handled. Test important actions before using them to judge a campaign. Do not assume an installed tracking tool is measuring correctly.

Plan a manageable test

Agree a budget you can afford to test, keep advertising spend separate from service fees, and decide what you want to learn. Prepare a few meaningful variations in the message or creative. Avoid changing everything at once, because it becomes harder to understand the findings.

Prepare the follow-up

Write a helpful initial response and decide how quickly your team can reply. Keep a simple record of enquiry quality and common questions. Platform activity is only part of the picture; the customer experience after the click matters too.

Before you launch

Review the offer, destination, access, tracking, creative, budget, and follow-up process together. Outcomes depend on your market, offer, budget, and execution. Use early findings to decide what to improve rather than treating the first campaign as a guaranteed source of sales.
